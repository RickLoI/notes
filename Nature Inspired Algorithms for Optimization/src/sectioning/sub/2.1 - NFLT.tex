    With little to no effort, it is easy to see that in real-life there's a lot of problems,
        and most of these are optimization ones.
        A question then follows: is there a best algorithm to solve all these problems?

    An answer to the question was given in \cite{WolpertM1997},
        in which the authors, through their \emph{No Free Lunch Theorem},
        show how there cannot be a best algorithm that solves all optimization problems.
        The idea behind the proof is the following: let $A_{1}$ and $A_{2}$ be two algorithms
        that solve an optimization problem. If $A_{1}$ outperforms $A_{2}$,
        then there must exist another optimization problem for which $A_{2}$ outperforms $A_{1}$.
