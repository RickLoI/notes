    Evaluating polynomial in a projective space is tricky, 
        depending on the polynomial at hand.
        Consider a polynomial in which its monomyals have different degrees,
        take for instance the following:
        \[
            f(X) = f(x_{0}, x_{1}, x_{2}) = x_{0} - x_{2}x_{1}.
        \]
        We would expect that, evaluating it a the points \(P = [2, -1, 1]\) and \(Q = 3P = [6, -3, 3]\),
        which are equal under the homogeneous interpretation,
        we would get the same result.
        Instead we get 
        \[
            f(P) = 2 - (-1 * 1) = 3 \text{ and } f(Q) = 6 - (-3 * 3) = 15.
        \]

    As the reader might have understood, the issue is that polynomials like the one above 
        are not homogeneous. That is, they don's satisfy the following equation:
        \[
            f(\lambda x_{0}, \lambda x_{1}, \lambda x_{2}) = \lambda^{d} f(x_{0}, x_{1}, x_{2})
        \]
        where \(d\) is the degree of the monomyals.
    
    Let \(f(X)\) and \(g(X)\) be two homogeneous polynomials,
        and let \(d\) be the degree of their monomyals.
        Let us see what happens when we compute \(F(X) = \sfrac{f(X)}{g(X)}\);
        we get 
        \[
            F(\lambda X) = \frac{f(\lambda X)}{g(\lambda X)} = 
                \frac{f(\lambda x_{0}, \lambda x_{1}, \lambda x_{2})}{g(\lambda x_{0}, \lambda x_{1}, \lambda x_{2})} = 
                \frac{\lambda^{d} f(x_{0}, x_{1}, x_{2})}{\lambda^{d} g(x_{0}, x_{1}, x_{2})} = 
                F(X).
        \]
        That is, \(F(X)\) doesn't care about multiplicative constants.

    Luckly for us, in the projective space 
        any polynomial can be seen as the ratio of two homogeneous polynomials.
        For instance, the following non-homogeneous polynomial
        \[
            F(x, y) = 3x^{2} -2y + 1 
        \]
        can be transformed into an homogeneous polynomial as follow:
        we replace \(x\) by \(\sfrac{x_{1}}{x_{0}}\) and \(y\) by \(\sfrac{x_{2}}{x_{0}}\),
        compute the lowest common multiple and obtain
        \[
            F(x, y) = 3x^{2} - 2y + 1 = \frac{3x_{1}^{2} - 2x_{0}x_{2} + x_{0}^{2}}{x_{0}^{2}} = F(x_{0}, x_{1}, x_{2}).
        \]
        Evaluating \(F(x_{0}, x_{1}, x_{2})\) at \(P\) and \(Q\), we get the same result of \(\sfrac{3}{4}\).
