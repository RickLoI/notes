    Named after the British mathematician John Pollard,
        the two algorithms presented in this section allow,
        under certain hypotesis, to find one of the prime factors of a composite integer \(n\).
        
    The first of the two algorithms we present is \emph{Pollard's \(p - 1\) factoring algorithm}.
        The algorithm is based on the notion of \(B\)-smoothness,
        which we define below.

    \begin{definition*}[B-smoothness]
        Let \(n\) be a composite integer, and let \(B\) a fixed bound.
            We say \(n\) is \(B\)-smooth, if all the prime factors of \(n\) are less or equal to \(B\).
    \end{definition*}
    For instance, \(n = 60\) is \(5\)-smooth, since it's factors are \(2, 3, 2, 5\).
